# Cushion Support: native launch (3 ad sets × 3 ads = 9 creatives, 3 copies)

Built from Venalys's top 3 angles (finish line, expert confession, flare-ups). The creatives borrow the hooks that already work in Venalys's statics.

**Structure:**
| Ad set | Angle | Copy | Creatives |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Finish line | Copy A: "The receipt drawer" | 1A, 1B, 1C |
| 2 | Expert confession (her doctor) | Copy B: "I thought it was a scam" | 2A, 2B, 2C |
| 3 | Flare-ups never fixed | Copy C: "I stopped saying flare up" | 3A, 3B, 3C |

- Each ad set uses **one copy**. The 3 ads inside it differ **only in the creative**, so each set tells you which image wins for that angle.
- Every ad goes to the product page with the 5-month bundle preselected.
- The hook is the creative's text overlay plus the first line of the copy.

**Rules:**
- **Real people only.** Each copy is a template for a real customer or creator posting from their own page (a partnership ad).
- **No invented doctors, and no cure claims.**
- Fill in `[brackets]` with the real person's details. Asterisk the claims and add the FDA disclaimer.

## Ad set 1: Finish line

### Copy A: "The receipt drawer"
I added up every hemorrhoid receipt from the last 3 years. $[612]. Third flare this year.

I keep receipts. It's a habit from doing the books for my husband's shop. So when the third flare hit, I sat at the kitchen table and added up every tube, every box of wipes, every "maximum strength" anything.

And here's what got me: every single one of those products worked. For a day. Maybe two. Then I'd be back at the pharmacy, picking the box with the biggest number on it, praying the cashier wasn't someone from church.

That's the business model. If it actually fixed anything, you'd stop buying it.

So I stopped looking for a stronger cream and started looking for why it kept coming back. Everything on that shelf works on the surface. The swelling starts underneath, in the tissue cushions and the support around them, and that support weakens with age and with babies. I'd had three.

That's when I found [Cushion Support]. It's not a cream. It's two capsules a day, and the part that sold me is that it's a **program with an end date**: [5] months, not forever. It felt like the first product in this category that wasn't counting on me coming back.*

I'm on month [3]. I won't tell you it's a miracle. I'll tell you the drawer is empty and I haven't added a receipt since [month].*

If you've got a drawer like mine, look at this before you buy another tube 👇

## Ad set 2: Expert confession (retold by the patient)

### Copy B: "I thought it was a scam"
I screenshotted this ad to send to my sister as "look at this scam." Four months later I'm the one posting about it.

I have a rule: if an ad promises to fix something embarrassing, it's a scam.

Then my doctor said something at my follow-up that I haven't stopped thinking about. I'd asked about banding. She said, "We can band it. But if we don't do anything about why the tissue is weak, a lot of people end up back here."

A lot of people end up back here.

So I went home and read for two weeks, like a crazy person. The short version: those cushions down there are held in place by connective tissue, and it gets weaker after 40, after pregnancy, after years of straining. Creams soothe the surface. Banding removes what's there. Neither one does anything about the support.

That's what [Cushion Support] is built around: daily support for the tissue and veins, not the surface.* I still figured it was a scam. What got me was the guarantee: [90] days, and if it does nothing for you, you get your money back. So the worst case was I'd be right.

I was wrong. [Real result, in her words, e.g. "I sat through my son's whole graduation without shifting once."]*

I still think most ads are scams. This one just wasn't.

*Not medical advice. Talk to your own doctor, mine is the reason I looked into this.*

## Ad set 3: Flare-ups never fixed

### Copy C: "I stopped saying flare up"
Flare up. I said that word for 9 years before anyone told me what it actually means.

"It's just flaring up again." I said it to my husband when I bailed on the road trip. I said it to myself when I chose the aisle seat at every wedding.

Here's what nobody told me: something that comes and goes was never gone. A flare up isn't bad luck. It's the same weak spot giving out again.

Every time it calmed down, I thought I'd won. I'd stop the cream, forget about it, and three weeks later I'd be back to planning my whole day around where I could sit.

What changed for me was learning the difference between *calming it* and *supporting what's underneath*. The cushions and the tissue that holds them weaken with age and pregnancy, and the cream doesn't reach that.

I've been taking [Cushion Support] for [4] months. Two capsules a day. [Real result, e.g. "I haven't said the words 'flare up' since August."]*

If you're still saying it, this is for you.

## After launch
- **Days 2–4:** in each ad set, kill the weakest creative and put a new image under the same copy. Venalys runs this exact pattern: one script, many creatives.
- **Scaling:** duplicate the winning ad set's best creative onto a second real customer's page.
- **Next wave:** new ad sets for the smaller angles that made Venalys's top 10: sex and intimacy shame ("Babe, is this a hemorrhoid?") and relief testimonial ("F*ck you, hemorrhoids").
