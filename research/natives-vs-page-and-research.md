# Biora's natives vs. the Veltia page and our customer research

Oct 14, 2026. Compares [`biora-ads/README.md`](biora-ads/README.md) (the top 6 natives) with the live Veltia page copy (`templates/product.veltia-drops.json`) and our research (`customer-research-bootstrap.md`, `native-ad-research.md`, `unique-mechanisms.md`, `formula-and-copy-audit.md`).

## The headline finding

**Biora's natives sell to a different person than our page and research do.** All 6 are diarrhea and urgency stories: IBS-D, Imodium, bathroom mapping, a cruise "crisis". Our research says urgency is roughly 1 in 7 of the pain in the market, and bloating dominates (239 vs. 33 mentions). Our page is built for the bloater. Copying their stories as they are would send diarrhea traffic to a bloating page.

That's also the opportunity: **nobody is running Biora-style long-form natives for the bloater.**

## Where the natives are ahead of our page

1. **Being believed / doctor dismissal.** This is the spine of every Biora native, and in our research it's the #1 frustration word ("Doctors don't listen", "No answers", normal test results). **Our page never says it.** "Doctor" appears 6 times, but only as "see a doctor" safety lines. There's no "your tests came back normal and you were told to live with it" beat.
2. **One question that changes everything.** The natives give the reader the moment the story turns (the ship doctor's question, the neighbor's question). Our research says the reader is hungry for one coherent explanation. Our page states the mechanism but never dramatizes the discovery.
3. **Story vehicle.** Long first-person story, a specific scene, a P.S. callback. Our page is all benefit blocks and reviews.

## Where our page and research are ahead of the natives

1. **Appearance and the "pregnant by dinner" scene.** Our research rates this the most visual, scene-ready pain. Our page uses it ("Flat in the morning, bloated by dinner", the jeans review). **Biora's natives never touch it.**
2. **Safety and tolerability.** Side-effect fear is the #1 "hate" theme in the category. Our page has "start low, build up slowly" and a "Who should not take these drops?" FAQ. The natives skip it.
3. **Honest claims.** Our page uses asterisks and a disclaimer. The natives rely on invented doctors, "IBS isn't real", drug-harm stories and Bible cures. Those are the claims that get ad accounts banned and draw FTC and FDA action.

## Mismatches to fix before writing our natives

1. **The mechanism isn't the one our research picked.**
   - Our page leads with the biofilm "shield", which is garlic breaking the shield.
   - `unique-mechanisms.md` ranked **M1, the Methane Brake** (garlic's allicin vs. the gut-slowing methane makers) first, and **M7, the Stuck Loop**, as its problem-aware version.
   - `native-ad-research.md` flags biofilm as the community's secondary concept (29 docs vs. 119 for motility) and as a trigger for side-effect fear.
   - The page has **0 mentions of methane or constipation**, although that's the lane research said Biora doesn't own.
   - Pick one before writing the natives, so the ad and the page tell the same story. Either stay with "the shield" or switch both to the methane/"stuck" story.
2. **Two trust lines conflict with our own audit:**
   - **"Backed by 10,000+ Scientific Studies"** (the ingredients heading) was flagged as misleading when Biora used it. It counts all studies on the herbs, not this formula or dose.
   - **"Allergen-Free"**: wormwood is in the daisy/ragweed family, so people with ragweed allergy should be cautious. The badge is fine if it means "free of the major food allergens", but the FAQ should mention the ragweed-family caution.
3. **Demographic.** The natives speak as women 44–56. Research suggests women 25–45 for the bloating lead. Our reviewers are 38–45, which bridges the two. Write natives for about 35–55.

## What to borrow (structure, not claims)

| Biora native | Keep | Change for Veltia |
|---|---|---|
| #1 cruise story | A specific trip ruined by the gut, an outsider's single question, a P.S. before/after | Make it a **bloating** crisis: the dress that won't close at a wedding, "are you expecting?" on vacation. Use a real customer, or a clearly fictional dramatization labelled as such, never a fake doctor. |
| #2 insider confession | "I did everything right and it still didn't work" | Tell it as a real customer or the founder, not an invented GI specialist. |
| #5 misdiagnosis | "Maybe it was never what they told me" | Make it "I was told it's just bloating / just stress / just IBS, and my tests were normal", which is the research's own belief shift. Don't claim IBS isn't real. |
| #4 medication fear | "Why do I need this every day?" | Skip it. A drug-harm claim is high risk and doesn't fit bloaters. |
| #3 Bible cure | An ancient-wisdom, traditional-herbs angle | Possible as heritage ("herbs used for generations"), never as a "cure". |

## Suggested first three Veltia natives

1. **"Flat in the morning, pregnant by dinner."** Appearance and control, with a scene-led opening. Research H1 says this should win on CTR. It lands on the page's problem section.
2. **"It worked, then it came back."** Relapse, for people who have tried other fixes. The mechanism explains why it returns. It lands on the "Keeps Coming Back" headline.
3. **"My tests were normal."** Being believed, told as a real story. It fills the biggest gap that both the natives and our research point to.
