## How each one argues
**Cirae: "the exit is shut"**
- **Villain:** allopurinol, colchicine, the gout diet, cherry juice (29 g of sugar), and drugstore tart cherry (heat-processed, with no anthocyanin number on the label).
- **Mechanism:** gout isn't too much uric acid being made. Your kidney filters it, then pulls it back. Anthocyanins "open the exit."
- **Fear:** your kidney function quietly drops a point a year, leading to dialysis. "Nobody puts your kidney numbers side by side."
- **Proof:** a Boston University study of 633 people, where cherry extract meant a 45% lower risk of an attack, plus an NEJM allopurinol trial.
- **Level 3–4 pain:** "sat in the car in the parking lot because he couldn't walk 200 feet to watch our son play", "missed his brother's 50th".
- **Timeline:** week 1 ache quiet, week 3 no twinges, week 6 uric acid under 6.8, week 12 kidney number up.

**Venalys: "the collagen cushion collapsed"**
- **Mechanism:** everyone has 3 cushions held in place by collagen. After 40 you lose about 1% of collagen a year, so the cushions slip. Bleeding and itching are symptoms; the collapsed cushion is the cause.
- **Villain:** creams (hydrocortisone is only labeled for 7 days), fiber, wipes, donut cushions, homeopathics, **and vein supplements (diosmin, hesperidin, horse chestnut): "Tone the vessels, ignore the mesh."**
- **Hero ingredient:** **non-alkalized cacao flavanols**, presented as "the only ingredient aimed at the cause."
- **Offer hook:** a finish line. "Stop after 150 days and stay hemorrhoid-free," backed by a 12-month guarantee.
- **Level 3–4 pain list:** "a donut cushion in your trunk, under a towel, and nobody knows", "you canceled the road trip to see family last year. And the year before that."

## What matters for you
1. **Your template's formula is Venalys's villain.** I built `cushion-support` around diosmin, hesperidin and horse chestnut. Venalys's listicle names exactly those as "vein support isn't repair." Two options:
   - Match the cacao-flavanol plus collagen mechanism, or
   - Position against it: "vein support plus collagen, both ends."

   Either way, update the ingredient cards to match whatever you source.
2. **Venalys wins on average order value.** $179.50 for the 5-bottle program against Cirae's $99.99. The 150-day "finish line" is what justifies 5 bottles. Copy the program structure, not the cure claim.
3. **Cirae wins on value framing.** $20 a bottle, "no subscription", and "feel better or it's free" are a strong conversion stack for men 45+.
4. **Both use invented doctor personas** ("Dr. Rachel Hayes, MD" and "Dr. Matthew Hayes, MD", the same surname, possibly the same copywriter) and comments that look made up.
   - This is the highest-risk part of both funnels: FTC rules on deceptive endorsements, Meta's policies, and your chargeback rate.
   - Use the **structure** (story, villain, mechanism, timeline, offer) with real people or a clearly labeled brand voice.
5. **Disease claims:** Cirae ("stops gout attacks", "kidney function back out of stage 3") and Venalys ("restores your rectal cushion", "stay hemorrhoid free") both go well past supplement claims. If you copy them, you inherit the risk.
