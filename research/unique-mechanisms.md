### M1. The Methane Brake
- **Big idea:** *Garlic's allicin stops the bugs that make gut-slowing methane gas.*
- **UMP:** the missing piece is that one gut gas, **methane**, isn't just gas. Studies show it slows the gut's pushing muscle, so stool sits, ferments and bloats her by evening (Pimentel 2006, *Am J Physiol GI*: methane slowed small-bowel transit; Kunkel 2011, *Dig Dis Sci* meta-analysis: methane linked to constipation). Fiber, Miralax and Linzess push from behind while the brake stays on. That's why being told "they all day to use miralax or eat more fiber" (Post 5) never fixed it.
- **UMS:** allicin (from the garlic) acts on the methane-making microbes so the brake comes off. Oregano's carvacrol is a second hit on the same gas makers. **PROVISIONAL:** needs allicin actually present, and the human data on allicin vs. methanogens is limited (clinician-reported, to verify).
- **Gradualization chain:**
  1. Gut bugs make gas when they eat what you eat.
  2. Most of that gas just leaves your body.
  3. One gas, methane, does something extra.
  4. Methane makes the gut muscle push slower.
  5. When the gut pushes slower, stool sits longer.
  6. Stool that sits makes more gas and more bloat.
  7. Laxatives push harder but the slow-down gas is still there.
  8. Take away the methane, and the gut can move at its own pace again.
- **Stage:** solution aware. It explains why the laxatives, fiber and diet she's comparing don't stick.
- **Belief fit:** **rides HB1 + HB2.** Reframes E2 ("it comes back") as *the brake was never removed*. Watch HB6 (garlic trigger) and HB7 (sulfur).

### M2. The Archaea Blind Spot
- **Big idea:** *Allicin reaches the gas makers that bacteria killers miss.*
- **UMP:** the gas makers behind methane bloat aren't bacteria at all. They're **archaea** (mainly *Methanobrevibacter smithii*), a separate branch of life. Probiotics, "kill the bacteria" herbs and Biora's whole story are aimed at bacteria. That's why the methane type keeps coming back. Guidelines pair a second antibiotic (neomycin) with rifaximin for methane for a reason (ACG 2020).
- **UMS:** allicin acts on the methane-making archaea; **PROVISIONAL** as in M1.
- **Gradualization chain:**
  1. Most gut fixes are made to hit bacteria.
  2. Doctors even add a second antibiotic for the methane type.
  3. That's because methane makers aren't bacteria.
  4. They are archaea, a different kind of tiny life.
  5. A tool built for bacteria can miss them.
  6. So the gas comes back after a "bacteria" fix.
  7. Allicin from garlic is the herb people use to go after the methane makers.
- **Stage:** **product aware** (out-positions Biora's bacteria-only page) and solution aware.
- **Belief fit:** **rides HB1 + HB2** (archaea itself is named in only 19 docs, so semi-known). Reframes E1 ("tried everything") as *everything was aimed at bacteria*.

### M3. The Leftover Yeast
- **Big idea:** *Oregano oil clears the yeast that antibiotics leave behind.*
- **UMP:** antibiotics and bacteria-only herbs clear bacteria but don't touch **yeast (Candida)**. With the bacteria gone, yeast has room to grow, and bloating returns weeks later. She reads that as "it came back," but it may be a different bug. Community: *"Majority of people have mixed SIBO/SIFO which is why SIBO treatments don't work."* (comment nyp27sq). SIFO is a recognized entity (to verify: Rao & Bhagatwala 2019 review).
- **UMS:** carvacrol (oregano) and allicin (garlic) both have antifungal action on Candida. **PROVISIONAL:** that evidence is mostly lab-based; the human gut data is thin.
- **Gradualization chain:**
  1. Antibiotics are made to kill bacteria.
  2. Your gut also has yeast in it.
  3. Antibiotics do nothing to yeast.
  4. When bacteria die off, yeast gets more room.
  5. More yeast can mean more gas and bloat.
  6. So it feels like "it came back" even after a good round.
  7. Oregano oil is one of the few herbs known to work on yeast.
- **Stage:** solution aware (post-antibiotic relapsers).
- **Belief fit:** **rides HB3**. Reframes E2. Doesn't fight anything.

### M4. The Signal Jammer
- **Big idea:** *Garlic's ajoene jams the signal gut bugs use to build their shield.*
- **UMP:** bugs don't build a protective biofilm alone. They wait until they "hear" enough neighbors through a chemical signal (**quorum sensing**) and build together. Breaking a shield (Biora's clove pitch) still leaves them able to rebuild it.
- **UMS:** **ajoene**, a garlic compound, blocks quorum-sensing signals in lab studies (Jakobsen 2012, *Antimicrob Agents Chemother*, in *Pseudomonas*). **PROVISIONAL:** ajoene only forms in oil-based garlic preparations, and there's no gut-specific human data.
- **Gradualization chain:**
  1. Gut bugs can hide under a slimy shield.
  2. You already know that shield protects them.
  3. Bugs only build it when enough of them gather.
  4. They count each other with a chemical signal.
  5. No signal, no shield.
  6. A garlic compound called ajoene blocks that signal.
- **Stage:** product aware (directly counters Biora's "dissolve the shield").
- **Belief fit:** **rides HB4**. Risk: abstract; the evidence is far from her gut.

### M5. The One-Lock Problem
- **Big idea:** *Bugs that dodge one herb can't dodge this whole blend.*
- **UMP:** single-herb protocols fail when part of the overgrowth is **resistant to that one herb**. Community: *"most of the bacteria that are overgrown in my gut are resistant to most commonly used herbal antimicrobials, like oregano and berberine"* (comment mb5ny28).
- **UMS:** carvacrol breaks the bug's outer wall; allicin disables its sulfur-based enzymes; juglone adds a third route. Different targets, so one resistance trick doesn't cover them all. **PROVISIONAL:** lab mechanisms only, doses unknown, the other 4 botanicals unknown.
- **Gradualization chain:**
  1. Some bugs can shrug off one herb.
  2. That's why one herb can work for a friend but not for you.
  3. Each herb hits bugs in its own spot.
  4. Oregano breaks the wall.
  5. Garlic's allicin jams the bug's tools.
  6. A bug rarely has a defense for both at once.
- **Stage:** solution aware.
- **Belief fit:** rides E1 / E3 ("herbs didn't work"). Close to "more ingredients = better" positioning; the villain is generic.

### M6. The Die-Off Flood
- **Big idea:** *Starting drop by drop stops the die-off flood that made you quit.*
- **UMP:** she didn't fail because herbs don't work. A full-strength first dose kills a lot of bugs at once. Their breakdown products (**endotoxin / LPS**) flood out and cause the nausea, headaches and cramps she blamed on the herb, so she stopped early. *"Oil of oregano made me so sick. Nausea, headaches, diarrhea, irritability, hot flashes. I suffered thru for two weeks and had to stop"* (odpdqaw); *"I recently started oregano and allicin, separately and half dose to then full dose."* followed by *"have been super nauseous"* (1wsf1x7).
- **UMS:** the **dropper** lets her start at a few drops and climb, which capsules can't do. **PROVISIONAL:** die-off is anecdotal in SIBO, and your product still contains the same antimicrobials.
- **Gradualization chain:**
  1. When gut bugs die, they break open.
  2. What spills out can make you feel sick.
  3. A big first dose breaks open a lot at once.
  4. That's the "die-off" people talk about.
  5. Most people quit right there.
  6. A dropper lets you start small and build up.
- **Stage:** solution aware.
- **Belief fit:** **rides HB5 and E3/E11** without asking her to drop them. Doesn't deliver Control by itself.

### M7. The Stuck Loop (M1 for the problem-aware)
- **Big idea:** *Cutting methane breaks the loop that keeps your gut stuck.*
- **UMP:** her "food sits there" bloating feeds itself. Slow gut → stool sits → microbes make more methane → gut slows more (Pimentel 2006 as above). That's why she's flat in the morning and swollen by dinner, no matter how "clean" she eats.
- **UMS:** allicin + carvacrol cut the methane side of the loop. **PROVISIONAL** as M1.
- **Gradualization chain:**
  1. Your stomach is flat in the morning.
  2. By dinner it's swollen.
  3. That's food sitting too long.
  4. Food that sits makes methane gas.
  5. Methane slows your gut even more.
  6. So each day starts the loop again.
  7. Stop the methane, and the loop has nothing to run on.
- **Stage:** **problem aware** (points her to the category: target the gas maker, not the food).
- **Belief fit:** rides the "effort without reward" belief (Posts 3, 16) and HB1.

### M8. The Hydrogen Hand-Off
- **Big idea:** *Oregano starves the methane makers by cutting off their hydrogen.*
- **UMP:** methane makers can't work alone. They eat **hydrogen** made by fermenting bacteria. That's why some people test "methane" after a hydrogen round.
- **UMS:** carvacrol reduces the hydrogen-producing bacteria, which starves the archaea of their fuel. **PROVISIONAL:** the evidence is lab and rumen work.
- **Gradualization chain:**
  1. Some gut bugs make hydrogen gas.
  2. Methane makers eat that hydrogen.
  3. No hydrogen means no methane.
  4. Oregano knocks down the hydrogen makers.
  5. The methane makers lose their food.
- **Stage:** solution aware (SIBO-literate).
- **Belief fit:** rides HB1. Two-step and indirect, so a harder sell in 5 sentences.
